# Claim-to-Evidence Map

## Purpose

This map links the manuscript's high-level framework claims to clusters within the retained literature shortlist. It is intended as a transparent review aid rather than a substitute for full manuscript argumentation.

## Claim 1: SMEs face strong pressure to digitally transform, but transformation trajectories are uneven

**Supporting theme(s):** SME digital transformation; readiness and maturity

**Representative papers:**
- Digital transformation success under Industry 4.0: a strategic guideline for manufacturing SMEs
- Drivers of Digital Transformation in SMEs
- Digital transformation and European small and medium enterprises (SMEs): A comparative study using digital economy and society index data
- Internationalization, digitalization, and sustainability: Are SMEs ready?

**Why this cluster matters:**
These papers establish the manuscript's baseline claim that SME transformation is both necessary and uneven, with observable differences in readiness, capability depth, and adoption conditions.

---

## Claim 2: Cybersecurity becomes more salient as SMEs digitize, but security is rarely integrated into stage-aware transformation guidance

**Supporting theme(s):** cybersecurity implications; readiness and maturity; SME digital transformation

**Representative papers:**
- Digital transformation and cybersecurity challenges for businesses resilience: Issues and recommendations
- Digital Transformation and Cybersecurity Challenges for Businesses Resilience: Issues and Recommendations
- Drivers of digital transformation in SMEs
- Sustainable industrial and operation engineering trends and challenges Toward Industry 4.0: a data driven analysis

**Why this cluster matters:**
These papers justify treating cybersecurity as a central implication of digital transformation while also showing that existing guidance remains broad, generalized, or only partially stage-aware.

---

## Claim 3: Resilience and continuity are important outcomes of SME transformation choices

**Supporting theme(s):** resilience and continuity; SME digital transformation

**Representative papers:**
- COVID-19 crisis and SMEs responses: The role of digital transformation
- SMEs and digital transformation during a crisis: The emergence of resilience as a second-order dynamic capability in an entrepreneurial ecosystem
- Building organizational resilience with digital transformation
- COVID-19 and digitalization: The great acceleration

**Why this cluster matters:**
This evidence cluster supports the manuscript's argument that transformation decisions are not only about efficiency or innovation; they also shape continuity, adaptation, and resilience under disruption.

---

## Claim 4: Resource and governance limits constrain what SMEs can prioritize and sustain

**Supporting theme(s):** resource or governance constraints; readiness and maturity

**Representative papers:**
- Drivers of Digital Transformation in SMEs
- COVID-19 crisis and SMEs responses: The role of digital transformation
- Economic business sustainability and strengthening human resource capacity based on increasing the productivity of small and medium enterprises (SMES) …
- The role of circular economy principles and sustainable-oriented innovation to enhance social, economic and environmental performance: Evidence from Mexican SMEs

**Why this cluster matters:**
These papers ground the manuscript's resource-constrained framing by showing that financial limits, staffing constraints, and governance conditions affect practical adoption and prioritization choices.

---

## Claim 5: The literature supports a bounded opportunity for a capability-prioritization framework

**Supporting theme(s):** all five themes, with strongest emphasis on readiness and maturity, cybersecurity implications, resilience and continuity, and resource or governance constraints

**Representative papers:**
- Digital transformation success under Industry 4.0: a strategic guideline for manufacturing SMEs
- Digital Transformation and Cybersecurity Challenges for Businesses Resilience: Issues and Recommendations
- Drivers of Digital Transformation in SMEs
- SMEs and digital transformation during a crisis: The emergence of resilience as a second-order dynamic capability in an entrepreneurial ecosystem

**Why this cluster matters:**
Taken together, the retained literature supports the manuscript's framing gap: the field contains useful ingredients, but lacks a clearly bounded, stage-aware, resource-sensitive capability prioritization logic tailored to SMEs.
