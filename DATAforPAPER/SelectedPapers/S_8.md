# The Impact of ESG Firms and Greenwashing Firms on Cost of Debt

Kuei-Yuan Wang, Ying-Li Lin<sup>(✉)</sup>, and Ruei-Chen Tsai

Department of Finance, Asia University, Taichung, Taiwan
{yuarn,yllin}@asia.edu.tw, 110035027@live.asia.edu.tw

**Abstract.** This study established its own greenwashing behavior indicators with reference to the KLD database and used regression analysis to explore the impact of ESG firms and greenwashing firms on cost of debt. The empirical results show that the impact of ESG firms on cost of debt is not significant, and the effect of greenwashing firms on cost of debt is also not significant. This empirical result means that when banks provide financing to firms, they do not obviously give firms better interest rates. This study not only fills the gap in the literature on greenwashing through the self-constructed greenwashing indicator but also provides a true picture of greenwashing among listed firms. In addition, authorities are reminded to pay more attention to the ESG performance of firms and curb the occurrence of greenwashing.

## 1 Introduction

Under the impact of the Covid-19 epidemic, investors have begun to pay attention to companies with higher ESG scores. The public demand for ESG disclosure continues to rise, and companies are making concerted efforts to integrate environment (E), society (S), and governance (G) into their core strategies to communicate their sustainable brand image [1, 5]. In addition, ESG investing is increasingly believed to improve portfolio performance and reduce portfolio risk [9]. In this atmosphere, some companies or organizations that do not take real green actions gain a green image that is full of environmental responsibility by spreading false green environmental information. This behavior is called “greenwashing” [10]. In fact, if a company engages in greenwashing to deceive investors, it will greatly damage the interests of investors.

[3] pointed out that when a company has good ESG performance, it can increase returns and reduce corporate risks, especially when the company faces a crisis, it will have time to respond and manage. This also indirectly reduces the risk of bank financing to enterprises. Therefore, under the government’s advocacy of sustainable finance, to encourage enterprises to implement ESG, the Financial Supervisory Commission coordinated banks to actively cooperate with the policies and preferential mechanisms of the competent authorities of various industries and handle related industry credit [4] to reduce the financing costs of enterprises. This study aims to examine whether companies committed to ESG can obtain cheaper funding costs, thereby demonstrating the incentive effect of the government in promoting green finance. This study also examines whether greenwashing can deceive financiers by examining whether greenwashing firms can obtain cheaper funding costs. This formed the motivation for this study.

The research objectives of this study include: (1) to examine whether better corporate ESG performance will significantly reduce the company’s financing costs; (2) to examine whether the degree of corporate greenwashing behavior will significantly affect the company’s average cost of capital; (3) to examine whether the financing costs of greenwashing companies are significantly cheaper than those of non-greenwashing companies in different dimensions.

The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) By testing whether the average funding cost of ESG companies is cheaper than that of non-ESG companies, we can test whether the Green Finance Action Plan really encourages ESG companies and provide the competent authorities with a way to observe the performance of their policies; (2) By referring to the ten dimensions of corporate green behavior in the KLD database, we have established a greenwashing enterprise index, which can not only fill the gap in domestic greenwashing related literature, but also make corporate green information more transparent; (3) By referring to the ten dimensions of corporate green behavior in the KLD database, we have established a greenwashing enterprise index; (4) By testing whether the average funding cost of greenwashing companies is cheaper than that of non-greenwashing companies, we can test whether banks can identify greenwashing companies when providing financing and lending; (5) We hope that through the results of this study, the competent authorities will be able to test which companies have greenwashing behavior while implementing policies, and through banks’ screening of preferential loan recipients, reduce the social costs caused by information asymmetry and implement sustainable finance.

## 2 Methodology

### 2.1 Hypotheses

[7] mentioned that countries or regions with higher ESG can effectively reduce systemic risks and enhance corporate value, and due to the increase in capital transparency, companies will have better funding channels. [2] mentioned that companies with higher levels of corporate social responsibility participation have better credit ratings than other companies that do not participate in corporate social responsibility, so it is relatively easy to obtain funds, thereby reducing the company’s capital costs; and ESG is an indicator for measuring corporate social responsibility; therefore, this study derives Hypothesis 1:

> **Hypothesis 1:** *The higher the ESG score of a company, the lower its financing costs.*

[11] found that banks in Taiwan regard ESG environmental disclosure as one of the important factors affecting loan interest rates. The reason is that by increasing information transparency, the risk of information asymmetry when banks lend is reduced, making banks willing to give ESG companies more favorable interest rates; therefore, to obtain better loan interest rates, [1] mentioned that companies will use greenwashing activities to change the views of stakeholders. Therefore, this study inferred hypothesis 2:

> **Hypothesis 2:** *The higher the level of corporate greenwashing, the lower the financing cost of the company.*

Since this study measures greenwashing enterprises in ten dimensions. Based on the hypothesis that enterprises will reduce their financing costs due to greenwashing, it is further believed that under different dimensions of measuring greenwashing enterprises, the financing costs of greenwashing enterprises will be significantly cheaper than the financing costs of non-greenwashing enterprises in the dimension, forming the third hypothesis of this study:

> **Hypothesis 3:** *Under different dimensions, the financing cost of greenwashing enterprises will be significantly cheaper than the financing cost of non-greenwashing enterprises in the same dimension.*

### 2.2 Empirical Period, Samples and Data Source

This study uses listed companies that have published sustainability reports in 2022 as samples. To determine whether the research samples of this study are green enterprises and greenwashing enterprises, this study collected information such as sustainability reports, annual reports, company official websites, and news reports for analysis. This study obtained sustainability reports and annual reports from public information observatories. News reports are to be obtained from the United News Knowledge Base. Financial report data were obtained from the Taiwan Economic Journal Database (TEJ). ESG information is obtained from the company’s investor relations integration platform (TDCC IR Platform).

### 2.4 ESG and Greenwashing Measurement

This study refers to the evaluation results of SUSTAINALYTICS ESG selected by [8]. Therefore, this study uses the SUSTAINALYTICS ESG risk score database in the company’s investor relations integration platform (TDCC IR Platform) as the ESG measurement indicator for this study; its score ranges from 0–100 points, with 0 being the best.

[8] divided greenwashing behaviors into 10 items based on the KLD database. If the advantages and disadvantages of each project exist at the same time, it means that the company is engaging in greenwashing. With each item worth 1 point, the company can get a maximum of 10 points for greenwashing. This study refers to [8] and [6] and modifies the operational definitions of the variables of the 10 items of greenwashing behavior in cost research, as shown in Table 1:

**Table 1.** Summary of definitions of advantages and disadvantages of each item of greenwashing behavior

| Greenwashing | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Community Relations | Volunteer service or community involvement projects in the community | Has been involved in tax evasion disputes within five years |
| Diversity | Have a diverse workforce | Controversial behavior that discriminates or divides based on race or gender |
| Industrial Relations | Develop good relationship between company and employees | There are disputes in labor relations |
| Employee Health | Have a complete employee health and safety system | Controversy over employee health and safety |
| Human Rights | Have excellent human rights disclosure | Controversial human rights violations in operations |
| Aboriginal Relations | Have good relations with the indigenous peoples nearby where you operate | Controversy over Aboriginal issues |

## 4 Conclusions and Suggestions

### 4.1 Conclusions

This study used Taiwan’s listed OTC companies in 2022 as sample to explore whether ESG firms and greenwashing firms will affect the cost of debt financing. Empirical results were as follows:

First, the ESG score did not significantly affect the debt financing costs of enterprises, indicating that Hypothesis 1 was not supported. The reason might be that ESG score had not yet been regarded as an important financing reference indicator by Taiwanese banks. Secondly, there was no significant impact between greenwashing and debt-financing costs, which means that although greenwashing occurs in Taiwan’s listed firms, it does not affect banks’ financing of enterprises.

Through the self-built greenwashing database, we can not only fill the gap in domestic greenwashing literature, but also further understand the actual situation of greenwashing in firms in Taiwan that have disclosed sustainability reports. It also reminds banks and management authorities to pay more attention to the ESG performance of companies, and at the same time pay attention to and curb the occurrence of greenwashing.

### 4.2 Suggestions

Since Taiwan does not have a unified greenwashing database, this study collected official and private data as a reference for the greenwashing database of this study. Since the process is quite time-consuming, only the data from 2022 was used as the sample of this study, which is one of the limitations of the study. Furthermore, since the evaluation results of SUSTAINALYTICS ESG cover the largest number of Taiwanese firms, but the actual number is only more than 400, it cannot fully reflect the current situation of Taiwan’s listed firms, which is the second limitation of this study.

### 4.3 Limits

The empirical results of this study found that ESG firms and greenwashing firms did not significantly affect the debt financing costs, which is very meaningful. First, the higher the ESG level of a firm, the lower its risk, which is very helpful for banks in assessing financing risks. Therefore, this study recommends that the government and banks should recognize the importance of ESG and actively and truthfully disclose corporate information. In addition, this study also suggests that investors should pay attention to the ESG status of a firm to determine whether the firm presents additional investment risks.

This study is limited by limited resources and the fact that many firms have not yet published sustainability reports and other information. Therefore, it is recommended that future scholars and the government establish unified ESG standards and force firms to publish truthfully so that future research can have a more realistic understanding of the profile of Taiwanese firms.
