### Abstract

**Purpose** – The purpose of this study is to examine the effect of Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) performance on the financial outcomes and cost of capital in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).

**Design/methodology/approach** – Using a data set of 227 SMEs from 2015 to 2020, this research uses multiple linear regression to assess the relationship between ESG scores, financial performance (Return on Assets and Return on Equity) and capital structure (weighted average cost of capital, cost of equity and cost of debt).

**Findings** – Higher ESG scores are linked to improved financial performance and reduced cost of capital, showing that ESG practices positively influence SMEs’ financial health.

**Research limitations/implications** – This study is limited to SMEs in specific regions and a five-year time frame. Future research could broaden the analysis across different regions or industries.

**Practical implications** – Managers should prioritize ESG initiatives to enhance profitability and reduce financing costs. Investors can use ESG scores to better assess SME investment risk.

**Social implications** – By integrating ESG practices, SMEs contribute to broader societal goals such as environmental sustainability and social responsibility, benefiting both stakeholders and communities.

**Originality/value** – This research uniquely focuses on the often-underexplored SME sector, providing fresh insights into how ESG strategies can improve financial outcomes and optimize capital costs.

**Keywords** ESG, Financial performance, SMEs, Cost of capital

**Paper type** Research paper

### Introduction

In the past decade, companies, investors and consumers around the world have been paying more attention to Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) dimensions, referred to by the abbreviation ESG (Dinh *et al.*, 2021; Alshehhi *et al.*, 2018). ESG parameters, determined by specific measures, are considered indicators of a firm’s non-financial performance, encompassing aspects such as climate change, social inequality, work environment, corporate governance and sustainability (Dinh *et al.*, 2021). The increasing importance of these factors has led to the rise of ESG rating agencies (Escrig-Olmedo *et al.*, 2019). ESG scores, calculated individually for ESG factors, provide a holistic measure of how a company performs in terms of sustainability and ethics (Arora and Sharma, 2022). This growing emphasis is particularly critical for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), which face unique challenges in resource allocation, competitive positioning and market visibility, making the integration of ESG practices a distinctive lever for enhancing their resilience and financial sustainability. As a result, companies have begun integrating these factors into financial analyses, recognizing their growing influence on investment choices and business decisions (Sahoo and Kumar, 2022; Alda, 2021; López-Felices *et al.*, 2023; Koraus *et al.*, 2015; Břečka and Koraus, 2016).

Investors and lenders now closely examine ESG ratings when assessing investments, considering both non-financial performance and financial risks and returns (Karlsson and Grundberg, 2022; Dinh *et al.*, 2021; Lingnau *et al.*, 2022; Boffo and Patalano, 2020; Bai *et al.*, 2023; Dobrovič and Koraus, 2015). This trend highlights the significant role ESG ratings play in shaping investment decisions and financial markets. ESG ratings also hold considerable importance in managerial decision-making, as firms increasingly direct investments toward achieving a balance between financial objectives and sustainability goals. Corporate sustainability offers a long-term strategic approach, blending environmental, societal and governance initiatives to enhance relationships with stakeholders and promote responsible practices that contribute to financial performance (Eccles *et al.*, 2014). This approach helps mitigate risks, identify opportunities and develop growth strategies that ultimately drive both financial success and sustainability. The rising demand for ESG data is driven by growing investor interest in how ESG factors influence investment decisions. Emerging social and ethical issues directly impact a company’s financial health and shareholder value, with calculated ESG performance data placing companies under heightened external scrutiny (Shakil *et al.*, 2019; Barko *et al.*, 2022; Schaltegger and Hörisch, 2017; Lee *et al.*, 2023; Diaz Tautiva *et al.*, 2023; Le *et al.*, 2023). Adapting to these demands, companies can gain competitive advantages, strengthening their brand and reputation (Raimo *et al.*, 2021). Transparent companies with high ESG ratings tend to attract investors, and managers can focus on improving non-financial performance to achieve financial goals (Siwiec and Karkowska, 2024; Ferreira *et al.*, 2024).

The purpose of this study is to examine the impact of ESG factors on a company’s financial performance and cost of capital. In particular, this research focuses on SMEs, clarifying how their unique operational characteristics and resource constraints shape the effects of ESG initiatives on financial outcomes and cost structures. Although numerous studies have explored the relationship between ESG practices and financial performance, there is still considerable debate, particularly regarding the effects on capital structure. Some studies suggest a positive relationship between ESG performance and financial outcomes (Ortas *et al.*, 2015; Velte, 2017; Minutolo *et al.*, 2019; Bhaskaran *et al.*, 2020; Buallay, 2019; Alareeni and Hamdan, 2020; Shaikh, 2022; Nguyen *et al.*, 2022; Jorgji *et al.*, 2024; Koundouri *et al.*, 2022; De Lucia *et al.*, 2020; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2021a; Cho *et al.*, 2024), with companies that prioritize ESG practices achieving better financial performance. However, studies examining the impact of ESG performance on capital structure have produced mixed results, with some indicating that companies with higher ESG ratings face increased costs of capital (Gjergji *et al.*, 2021), while others show reduced costs (Pellegrini *et al.*, 2019; Ng and Rezaee, 2015; Ramirez *et al.*, 2022; Piechocka-Kałużna *et al.*, 2021; Khanchel and Lassoued, 2022; Bussmann *et al.*, 2022; Ernst and Woithe, 2024; Priem and Gabellone, 2024; Yi *et al.*, 2020; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2021b), and some finding no significant relationship (Khanchel and Lassoued, 2022; La Rosa and Bernini, 2022).

This research aims to fill this gap by providing a comprehensive analysis of how ESG factors affect both financial performance and the cost of capital. Examining these relationships within the SME context, this study underscores the distinct contributions and challenges of ESG integration for smaller firms, which often differ from those observed in larger corporations. Unlike previous studies that have analyzed these elements separately, this study examines them together, offering a more complete picture of the role of ESG in corporate financial outcomes.

### Literature review

The relationship between ESG factors and corporate financial performance has been widely examined. This section provides a detailed review of theoretical and empirical research on how ESG practices influence both financial performance and the cost of capital, with particular attention to the unique challenges and contributions for SMEs.

#### Stakeholder theory
The Stakeholder Theory, as proposed by Freeman (1984), emphasizes the importance of managing relationships with customers, employees, suppliers, communities and shareholders. This perspective remains central in understanding how ESG practices can create value and mitigate risks. In the context of ESG, companies that address stakeholder concerns are better positioned to achieve long-term stability. Studies have documented that environmental measures can reduce regulatory risks and appeal to environmentally conscious communities (Dinh *et al.*, 2021; Duong, 2023; Gombár *et al.*, 2022; Koraus *et al.*, 2019), while social initiatives improve employee morale and customer loyalty (Alshehhi *et al.*, 2018). Governance practices ensure transparency and accountability, which further build stakeholder confidence (Escrig-Olmedo *et al.*, 2019). For SMEs, the emphasis on stakeholder engagement is especially critical because limited resources and local market dynamics mean that effective ESG practices can serve as a key strategy for maintaining competitiveness (Arora and Sharma, 2022).

#### Environmental, social and governance and financial performance
Research consistently indicates that firms which integrate ESG practices tend to achieve better financial outcomes. For example, evidence from studies on various markets has shown that ESG efforts are positively related to operational performance indicators such as Return on Assets (ROA) and Return on Equity (ROE) (Ortas *et al.*, 2015; Velte, 2017; Minutolo *et al.*, 2019). These studies suggest that investments in environmental improvements, social responsibility and sound governance can enhance overall profitability. Similarly, research on sectors ranging from banking to manufacturing confirms that companies disclosing ESG information often enjoy improved financial performance (Bhaskaran *et al.*, 2020; Buallay, 2019). Investigations focused on specific regions and markets, including studies by Alareeni and Hamdan (2020) and Shaikh (2022), further indicate that ESG practices can effectively reduce business risks and support robust financial growth. Moreover, the analysis of larger samples, such as those by Nguyen *et al.* (2022) and Jorgji *et al.* (2024), reinforces the view that ESG performance is instrumental in fostering profitability, particularly in firms facing resource constraints like SMEs. Koundouri *et al.* (2022), De Lucia *et al.* (2020), Chouaibi *et al.* (2021a) and Cho *et al.* (2024) provide additional evidence that comprehensive ESG strategies correlate with improved financial indicators, suggesting that the benefits of ESG extend across different operational dimensions.

#### ESG and cost of capital
Several studies have examined the link between ESG performance and financing costs, revealing that robust ESG practices tend to lower a company’s cost of capital. Research has shown that companies with strong ESG profiles experience reduced risks, leading to lower cost of equity (CoE) (Ng and Rezaee, 2015; Ramirez *et al.*, 2022), as investors perceive them as less risky. In similar fashion, studies indicate that a solid ESG record can result in lower cost of debt (CoD) and weighted average cost of capital (WACC) (Pellegrini *et al.*, 2019; Piechocka-Kałużna *et al.*, 2021). However, the literature presents mixed findings; for instance, Gjergji *et al.* (2021) report that in some cases, higher ESG ratings may be associated with increased capital costs for smaller firms. Additional research by Khanchel and Lassoued (2022), Bussmann *et al.* (2022), Ernst and Woithe (2024), Priem and Gabellone (2024) and Yi *et al.* (2020) supports the idea that improved ESG performance generally translates into lower financing costs, though La Rosa and Bernini (2022) note that the impact may vary with differences in disclosure quality and market expectations. These findings collectively suggest that for SMEs, where access to affordable financing is a persistent challenge, effective ESG management can not only contribute to better financial results but also enhance the terms and availability of external financing.

This review shown in Table 1 brings together the evidence that effective ESG implementation not only supports enhanced financial performance but also improves access to cost-effective financing options. The unique challenges faced by SMEs further emphasize the importance of tailored ESG strategies to achieve sustainable growth and maintain competitive advantage.

### Hypotheses development

#### ESG and financial performance
Several scholars (Ortas *et al.*, 2015; Velte, 2017; Minutolo *et al.*, 2019; Bhaskaran *et al.*, 2020; Buallay, 2019; Alareeni and Hamdan, 2020; Shaikh, 2022; Nguyen *et al.*, 2022; Jorgji *et al.*, 2024; Koundouri *et al.*, 2022; De Lucia *et al.*, 2020; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2022; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2021b; Cho *et al.*, 2024; Siwiec and Karkowska, 2024) have studied the relationship between ESG factors and financial performance, yielding mixed results. This section explores the key findings in this area. Ortas *et al.* (2015), Velte (2017) and Minutolo *et al.* (2019) revealed that ESG scores positively impact financial performance, specifically ROA and Tobin’s Q. For instance, Ortas *et al.* (2015) studied 198 companies from Spain, France and Japan that joined the UN Global Compact and found that ESG factors significantly enhanced ROA and Tobin’s Q. Similarly, Velte (2017) analyzed 412 German firms and found that ESG scores improved ROA, though the impact on Tobin’s Q was less pronounced. Governance factors had a particularly strong effect. Minutolo *et al.* (2019) also confirmed these positive effects using data from the S&P 500. Bhaskaran *et al.* (2020) and Buallay (2019) expanded on this by exploring different sectors. Bhaskaran *et al.* (2020) examined 4,886 firms and found that environmental factors contributed positively to financial performance, with variations across industries. Buallay (2019) analyzed 235 banks and confirmed that ESG disclosures positively affected ROA and ROE. However, the impact on Tobin’s Q was inconsistent. Alareeni and Hamdan (2020) also noted that ESG disclosures positively influenced financial metrics for US S&P 500 firms, while Mardini (2022) and Jorgji *et al.* (2024) highlighted the role of ESG in fostering stakeholder satisfaction. De Lucia *et al.* (2020) and Koundouri *et al.* (2022) focused on European companies. De Lucia *et al.* (2020) studied 1,038 firms and found that effective ESG practices improved financial health, reinforcing the positive relationship between ESG and financial metrics like ROA and ROE. Koundouri *et al.* (2022) examined companies in the euro Stoxx 50 index and similarly found a positive link between ESG performance and financial success. The role of ESG in innovation is another important theme. Chouaibi *et al.* (2022) examined ESG practices and green innovation in the UK and Germany, finding that companies adopting green innovation improved their financial performance. Chouaibi *et al.* (2021a) further investigated environmental reporting, showing a positive relationship between transparent ESG disclosures and financial performance, as measured by Tobin’s Q. Cho *et al.* (2024) confirmed these findings, showing that ESG performance positively correlates with firm value in Korean companies, though this effect can diminish during periods of poor financial performance.

Siwiec and Karkowska (2024) examined companies in Central and Eastern Europe and found a strong positive relationship between ESG practices and ROA. This suggests that firms in these regions benefit significantly from adopting ESG measures. Furthermore, Singhania *et al.* (2025) reviewed the increasing focus on ESG measurement, with data showing a rising trend in ESG-related research since 1992. The impact of governance structures has also been explored. García-Sánchez *et al.* (2022) studied 80 microfinance institutions (MFIs) in Bangladesh and found that governance, such as board size and CEO duality, played a crucial role in balancing corporate sustainability with social impact. Despite the consensus that ESG improves financial performance, the impact of specific ESG components varies. Environmental disclosures generally show the strongest effect, while social and governance factors may have mixed results. Based on this review, the following hypothesis is proposed:

**H1.** ESG ratings and their components positively affect the financial performance of companies, as measured by Return on Assets and Return on Equity.

#### ESG score and cost of capital
Previous studies (Pellegrini *et al.*, 2019; Fandella *et al.*, 2023; Ng and Rezaee, 2015; Ramirez *et al.*, 2022; Gonçalves *et al.*, 2022; Piechocka-Kałużna *et al.*, 2021; Khanchel and Lassoued, 2022; Bussmann *et al.*, 2022; La Rosa and Bernini, 2022; Gjergji *et al.*, 2021; Ernst and Woithe, 2024; Tian *et al.*, 2023; Priem and Gabellone, 2024; Yi *et al.*, 2020; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2021b; Said and ElBannan, 2024) have explored the relationship between ESG performance and cost of capital, generally supporting a negative association. Pellegrini *et al.* (2019) analyzed 182 publicly traded oil and gas companies from 2002 to 2018, finding that strong ESG performance reduced the CoE and improved ROA, showing how sustainability can lower financing costs. Fandella *et al.* (2023) studied the BRICS countries (Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa) from 2014 to 2019, discovering that ESG scores significantly reduced CoE and WACC, though the CoD remained unaffected. Similarly, Ng and Rezaee (2015) reviewed 3,000 firms from 1990 to 2013, revealing that firms with strong ESG performance tend to lower their CoE. Ramirez *et al.* (2022) looked at 202 Latin American firms and found that better ESG performance was associated with reduced capital costs. Gonçalves *et al.* (2022) confirmed this, showing that ESG performance reduced CoE for European companies in the STOXX Europe 600 index (2002–2018). However, they noted that CoD could increase with improved ESG ratings, highlighting that sustainability’s influence on cost structures might vary between financing channels. Piechocka-Kałużna *et al.* (2021) studied US companies from 2016 to 2020 and found a negative relationship between ESG scores and WACC and CoE, while CoD remained unaffected. Khanchel and Lassoued (2022) analyzed ESG disclosures among 430 US companies and found that environmental and governance disclosures reduced WACC in the early years, although social disclosures increased costs over time. Bussmann *et al.* (2022) expanded the analysis globally across 1,400 multinational companies in 43 countries, concluding that higher ESG scores generally resulted in lower WACC and CoE. La Rosa and Bernini (2022) examined controversies related to ESG disclosures and found that controversies had no significant impact on CoE for European companies, suggesting that controversies may not always harm capital costs. However, Gjergji *et al.* (2021) found contrasting results among 87 SMEs in Italy, where ESG disclosures increased WACC, likely because of higher scrutiny on smaller firms with limited resources. Ernst and Woithe (2024) studied 498 S&P 500 companies and discovered that high ESG scores reduced both CoE and CoD, while Tian *et al.* (2023) analyzed Chinese companies between 2009 and 2020, finding that improved ESG ratings led to lower CoD. Priem and Gabellone (2024) examined 600 companies across 17 European countries and found that strong ESG scores were associated with reduced capital costs, particularly in nations with weaker regulatory frameworks, where environmental and social factors had a larger impact. Yi *et al.* (2020) focused on Chinese firms and found that poverty alleviation and social responsibility initiatives significantly reduced CoE. Chouaibi *et al.* (2021a) studied the effect of COVID-19 on CoE for French ESG companies and found that corporate social responsibility initiatives lowered CoE during the pandemic, though CEO remuneration had no effect. These studies collectively suggest that ESG performance, particularly environmental and governance factors, tends to lower financing costs by reducing perceived risk for investors and lenders. However, the effect on CoD remains inconclusive, with some studies indicating a neutral or positive relationship. Based on these insights, the following hypothesis is proposed:

**H2.** ESG and its components negatively affect the cost of capital, including the weighted average cost of capital (WACC), cost of equity (CoE) and cost of debt (CoD).

#### Estimation method and model
This study used multiple linear regression analyses to test the validity of this study hypotheses. This statistical technique is used to explain a dependent variable in terms of one or more independent variables. The multivariate regression approach summarizes the data and predicts the value of the dependent variable based on the independent variables. It also identifies which independent variables have the most significant impact on the dependent variable (Tranmer and Elliot, 2008; Tabachnick and Fidell, 1983).

Multiple linear regression, which is a type of regression analysis that involves the use of multiple independent variables, has a general formula as follows:

$$Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_{i1} + \beta_2 X_{i2} + \beta_3 X_{i3} + \dots + \beta_k X_{ik} + \epsilon_i$$

In this equation, $Y$ represents the dependent variable, $X_{i1}$ to $X_{ik}$ represent the independent variables, $\epsilon_i$ represents the statistical error term, $\beta_0$ represents the regression constant and, $\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3, \dots, \beta_k$ represent the partial regression coefficients (Berry *et al.*, 1985). The multiple regression analysis used in this study can be represented as follows, based on the equation provided above:

$$Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_1 + \beta_2 X_2 + \beta_3 X_3 + \beta_4 X_4$$

In this study model, ROA, ROE, CoD, CoE and WACC were used as dependent variables expected to explain the hypotheses, and ESG scores and the three components of ESG were used as independent variables.

#### Dependent variables
This study has two main objectives:
1. The first is to determine the relationship between ESG scores and company performance.
2. The second is to examine the relationship between ESG scores and the cost of capital.

Accordingly, the dependent variables for the analysis have been selected. ROA and ROE were chosen because they are widely recognized measures of financial performance. Several studies, including those by Koundouri *et al.* (2022), Nguyen *et al.* (2022), Shaikh (2022), De Lucia *et al.* (2020), Alareeni and Hamdan (2020), Bhaskaran *et al.* (2020), Buallay (2019) and Shakil *et al.* (2019), have used ROA and ROE to assess the impact of ESG on financial performance (ROA; Cho *et al.*, 2024; Jorgji *et al.*, 2024; Siwiec and Karkowska, 2024; Ng and Rezaee, 2015), whereas CoE, CoD and WACC are included in the analysis to analyse whether companies are indeed rewarded with a lower cost of capital after achieving better ESG performance (Ernst and Woithe, 2024; Piechocka-Kałużna *et al.*, 2021; Fandella *et al.*, 2023; Khanchel and Lassoued, 2022; Ramirez *et al.*, 2022; COE, COD; Priem and Gabellone, 2024; COE; Ng and Rezaee, 2015; COD; Arora and Sharma, 2022).

#### Independent variables
For this study hypothesis testing, ESG scores obtained from Refinitiv were used as independent variables in this study. In addition, Environmental, Social and Corporate Governance scores were also used as independent variables to determine the effect of each of these factors and to analyse the result more comprehensively.

#### Control variables
To examine the influence of various factors on the relationship between ESG and financial performance and the cost of capital, this study includes several control variables that have been selected based on existing literature. The control variables related to financial performance were beta (Velte, 2017; Ng and Rezaee, 2015; Yu *et al.*, 2021; Gonçalves *et al.*, 2022; Yi *et al.*, 2020) and industry (Velte, 2017; Yi *et al.*, 2020; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2021b), whereas the control variables for cost of capital were ROA (La Rosa and Bernini, 2022; Ramirez *et al.*, 2022; Fandella *et al.*, 2023; Yi *et al.*, 2020) and ROE (Fandella *et al.*, 2023).

#### Industry dummy variable
The industry’s representation is captured using a simplified dummy variable based on the two categories defined in Table 4 (manufacturing and services). This simplification is justified by existing literature, which indicates that ESG effects are relatively homogeneous across industries (Velte, 2017; Yi *et al.*, 2020; Chouaibi *et al.*, 2021a).

#### Multicollinearity diagnostics
Multicollinearity among the independent variables was assessed using variance inflation factor and tolerance tests. Variance inflation factor values were found to be below the critical threshold (VIF < 10) and tolerance values above 0.1, confirming that multicollinearity is not a significant concern in this analysis.

#### Robustness checks
Robustness checks were conducted by incorporating additional control variables such as Tobin’s Q, firm size and leverage into the regression models. These checks confirmed that the relationships identified in the baseline model are stable and reliable.

#### The model
This research investigates how companies’ ESG performance affects their financial performance and cost of capital using two models.

(1) Model 1: ESG Performance Impact on Financial Performance (ROA and ROE)
*   In Model 1.1, financial performance is measured by ROA and, in Model 1.2, by ROE. Control variables include beta and the industry dummy variable.

(2) Model 2: The Impact of ESG Performance on Capital Costs (CoE, CoD and WACC)
*   In Model 2.1, the CoE is analyzed; in Model 2.2, the CoD is analyzed; and in Model 2.3, the WACC is analyzed. Control variables for Model 2 include ROA and ROE.

Models 1.1 and 1.2 test *H1*, while Models 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3 test *H2*.
The regression model is represented as:

$$FP_{it} = \alpha + \beta_1(ESG\_score)(it-1) + \beta_2(Environmental)(it-1) + \beta_3(Social)(it-1) + \beta_4(Governance)(it-1) + \beta_5(Control\_Variables)(it) + \epsilon_{it}$$

Here, $FP_{it}$ represents the financial performance (or cost of capital) of company $i$ at time $t$; $(ESG\_score)(it-1)$ is the overall ESG performance score at time $t–1$; $(Environmental)(it-1)$, $(Social)(it-1)$ and $(Governance)(it-1)$ are the individual ESG component scores at time $t–1$; $(Control\_Variables)(it)$ includes all relevant control variables; and $\epsilon_{it}$ is the error term.

### Sectoral and regional differences
Preliminary analysis indicates that while the overall impact of ESG on financial performance and capital costs is consistent across industries and regions, sectoral and regional differences exist. For instance, SMEs in manufacturing may experience different cost structures compared to those in services, and companies in more regulated regions might face higher initial costs for implementing ESG initiatives. These differences warrant further investigation to fully understand the heterogeneity of ESG effects across various contexts.

### Theoretical and managerial implications
From a theoretical perspective, the positive association between ESG performance and financial outcomes aligns with the Stakeholder Theory’s focus on balancing stakeholder interests (Barko *et al.*, 2022; Lee *et al.*, 2023). The theory explains that ESG initiatives help companies mitigate risks, enhance operational efficiency and improve corporate reputation, all of which contribute to better financial performance (Alda, 2021; Dinh *et al.*, 2021). Thus, the Stakeholder Theory not only offers a robust framework for understanding the financial benefits of ESG practices but also provides a foundation for future research into how sustainability shapes corporate strategy.

For managers, the study’s findings emphasize the importance of integrating ESG initiatives into corporate strategies to improve both financial performance and reduce capital costs. The positive relationship between ESG scores and ROA and ROE demonstrates that prioritizing stakeholder interests can improve profitability, especially for SMEs (Nguyen *et al.*, 2022; Koundouri *et al.*, 2022). Managers should consider ESG initiatives as not just compliance measures but also strategic tools for driving long-term growth, enhancing value beyond financial metrics and strengthening relationships with key stakeholders (Schaltegger and Hörisch, 2017). Furthermore, the study highlights that firms with strong ESG performance tend to enjoy a lower cost of capital, including CoE, CoD and WACC. This is particularly important for SMEs, where access to affordable financing can be a challenge (Ramirez *et al.*, 2022; Piechocka-Kałużna *et al.*, 2021). By improving ESG performance, firms can signal lower risk to investors and lenders, resulting in more favorable borrowing terms and equity financing conditions (Ng and Rezaee, 2015). For example, Ramirez *et al.* (2022) found that firms with high ESG ratings benefited from reduced CoD, as lenders viewed them as lower-risk borrowers. This highlights the role ESG can play in enhancing financial sustainability for firms. ESG initiatives also contribute to better risk management, which is critical for ensuring long-term value creation. The study’s findings suggest that companies engaged in robust ESG practices are better equipped to mitigate financial and non-financial risks, such as environmental liabilities or governance failures (Alda, 2021). This is especially important for SMEs, which often lack the resources to absorb significant risks. Through proactive risk management, firms can reduce volatility and improve stability, making them more appealing to investors and lenders (Karlsson and Grundberg, 2022). Finally, the growing importance of ESG for investors has significant implications for how managers engage with the investment community. Companies with strong ESG ratings are increasingly favored by investors who prioritize sustainability in their decision-making processes (Dinh *et al.*, 2021; Karlsson and Grundberg, 2022). This trend is particularly relevant for SMEs, which often depend on external financing for growth (Schaltegger and Hörisch, 2017). By enhancing ESG disclosures, managers can attract sustainability-conscious investors, thereby improving access to capital and strengthening corporate reputation (Boffo and Patalano, 2020). Moreover, effective ESG management offers firms a competitive advantage in the marketplace, fostering trust and loyalty among customers and investors alike (Raimo *et al.*, 2021).

In addition, robust ESG practices have significant societal implications. For instance, by committing to ESG initiatives, firms can contribute to job creation through investments in green technologies and sustainable operations, stimulate community development via local sourcing and corporate social responsibility programs and support environmental sustainability efforts that improve public health. Furthermore, effective ESG strategies enhance social infrastructure by promoting fair labor practices and strengthening community engagement, which collectively contribute to societal stability and economic development.

### Conclusion
ESG issues have become pivotal in shaping both investment strategies and corporate performance. Investors increasingly use ESG criteria – such as environmental impact, labor policies and governance practices – to assess companies’ long-term sustainability and ethical standards. These criteria are essential for securing investment and maintaining a positive reputation among stakeholders. This study investigates how ESG influences financial performance and the cost of capital in SMEs, focusing on key metrics like ROA, ROE and WACC.

The findings indicate that higher ESG scores improve financial performance, as reflected in ROA and ROE. This supports existing research, such as Buallay (2019) and Koundouri *et al.* (2022), which link strong ESG performance to increased profitability. Additionally, De Lucia *et al.* (2020) and Alareeni and Hamdan (2020) highlight positive correlations between ESG scores and financial indicators, further validating the results.

SMEs, in particular, exhibit unique contributions from ESG integration. Their reliance on sustainability and stakeholder trust makes ESG practices a vital tool for addressing local market challenges, fostering community development and enhancing operational resilience (Nguyen *et al.*, 2022; Jorgji *et al.*, 2024). This study also examined the link between ESG performance and the cost of capital, revealing that companies with high ESG scores enjoy lower financing costs, including reduced WACC, CoE and CoD. These results align with earlier studies by Ng and Rezaee (2015) and Pellegrini *et al.* (2019), which demonstrated a negative relationship between ESG scores and capital costs. However, discrepancies arise when compared with studies like Gonçalves *et al.* (2022), which showed a positive relationship between ESG scores and CoD. This highlights the complexity of how ESG impacts capital costs, especially in varying regulatory environments.

While this study adds valuable insights into ESG’s role in financial performance, it has limitations. The model’s explanatory power was constrained, likely because of limited data and analysis scope. Future research should incorporate more control variables and analyze a broader data set to enhance predictive accuracy. Additionally, this study focused on 227 companies over a five-year period, limiting its generalizability. Expanding the research to include different sectors and regions would help elucidate the unique contributions of ESG practices to SME performance and further clarify the societal benefits – such as job creation and community development – that arise from robust ESG integration.
