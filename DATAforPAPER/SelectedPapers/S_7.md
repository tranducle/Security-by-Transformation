# The impact of ESG performance on debt financing costs: Evidence from Chinese family business

**Wen Kong**
*School of Finance, Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics, Nanchang, Jiangxi 330013, China*

---

### ARTICLE INFO
**Keywords:**
* Family business
* ESG
* Debt financing costs
* Enterprise lifecycle

### ABSTRACT
In China, family business is an important part of private economy. This paper studies the impact of China’s family business’s ESG performance on debt financing costs. We find that: ESG performance of family business can obtain the support of creditors and reduce the cost of debt financing; The performance of family enterprises in social dimension and corporate governance dimension can reduce the debt financing cost of enterprises; ESG performance can significantly reduce the debt financing cost of family business when the family business is in the owner management stage, the children’s cultivation and development stage, and the power transfer stage.

---

## 1. Introduction

In 2015, the United Nations adopted 17 sustainable development goals required to be achieved by 2030 in the 2030 agenda for sustainable development. Enterprises are the main promoters of sustainable development. As the outside world pays more attention to the sustainable development of enterprises, it will price the sustainability practice of enterprises. As sustainable management becomes more and more important, existing enterprises begin to regard environmental, social and governance engagement (ESG) as an important business strategy internally.

In China, 85.4% of private enterprises are family enterprises, which create 75% of employment opportunities and contribute more than 50% of GDP. Due to the family participation and its relationship with the region, family business ESG has a unique role, but there are few studies on the performance of family business ESG. Berrone et al. (2010) pointed out that family businesses are more likely to meet the interests of different stakeholders. Kotlar and de massis (2013) stressed that, compared with non family businesses, the behavior of family businesses is affected by both economic and non economic goals, thus affecting the moral behavior of family businesses. Existing studies have mainly focused on the impact of ESG on stock returns (Zhang et al., 2021), financial performance (Chen et al., 2018) and enterprise innovation (Li et al., 2022). However, few studies have explored the impact of ESG performance on debt financing costs. Can ESG performance of family business get the support of creditors, thereby reducing the cost of debt financing? This is a matter of concern for family businesses. So this paper attempts to explore the impact of family business ESG performance on debt financing costs.

The research of this paper has important theoretical and practical significance. First, this paper focuses on China’s family businesses, studies the impact of family business ESG performance on debt financing costs, and enriches the relevant research of ESG (Wang et al., 2023; Kong et al., 2023; Kong et al., 2022). Second, we examine the impact of ESG sub items of family businesses on the cost of debt Financing, providing evidence of which aspect of ESG performance creditors value more. Third, we analyze whether the impact of ESG on debt financing costs is affected by the family life cycle, our findings have practical implications for family enterprises in different life cycles on how to use ESG to alleviate financing costs.

The rest of this paper is arranged as follows: Section 2 is theoretical analysis and research hypothesis; Section 3 is model setting and variable definition; Section 4 is the empirical result analysis; Section 5 is robustness analysis; Section 6 is further analysis; Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Theoretical analysis and research hypothesis

Compared with creditors, the managers of family businesses are the ones with information advantages. Information asymmetry between managers and creditors leads to adverse selection and moral hazard (Huang and Liu, 2020). Cho et al. (2013) believed that the disclosure of ESG report alleviated the problem of adverse selection between enterprises and stakeholders. Yang et al. (2018) found that ESG can significantly reduce the information asymmetry between enterprises and creditors. Hamrouni et al. (2019) believe that ESG disclosure can provide relevant financial information not reported in the financial statements, properly reflect ESG performance and make fund providers believe in the sustainability of the company, which may attract fund providers and promote debt financing of enterprises. Moreover, the disclosure of ESG information by family enterprises can improve their reputation and obtain social recognition, and reduce the compliance risk. When the compliance risk of the enterprise is higher than the average level of the market, creditors will demand higher interest as compensation, increasing the financing cost of the family business (Yang et al., 2022). Generally, creditors believe that companies with good ESG performance have lower risk level, so they require lower risk return. Based on the above analysis, this paper puts forward the following assumptions:

**H1: ESG performance of family business can reduce the cost of debt financing.**

## 3. Research design

### 3.1. Data

This paper selects the data of Chinese Shanghai and Shenzhen A-share listed family enterprises from 2009 to 2021. This paper mainly uses the definition of family enterprise by Xu and Wan (2019), that is: (1) The ultimate control right of the enterprise can be traced back to natural persons or families; (2) The ultimate controller of the enterprise is the largest shareholder of the enterprise and directly or indirectly holds the equity of the enterprise. After screening, 16,833 observation samples were finally determined, and the data came from CSMAR database, CNRDS database, Baidu and the financial statements of listed companies. ESG data is mainly selected from HUAZHENG ESG data. Other financial data are from CSMAR database. The data are processed as follows: (1) Financial family enterprises are excluded; (2) Eliminate ST, *ST and delisted family enterprises; (3) Eliminate samples with missing data; (4) All continuous variables were tailed up and down by 1%.

### 3.2. Variable definition

**(1) Explained variable**

Cost: Refer to Huang and Liu (2020) to measure the cost of corporate debt financing using interest expense / total liabilities. The greater the ratio of interest expenses to total liabilities, the higher the debt financing cost of enterprises.

**(2) Explanatory variable**

ESG: HUAZHENG ESG rating system has 16 themes and more than 40 key indicators, which can well evaluate the ESG disclosure of listed companies, and has been widely used in China’s ESG Research (Gao et al., 2021; Xi and Wang, 2022; Xie and Lv, 2022). The ESG rating index of HUAZHENG is C-AAA, with nine grades, assigned from 1 to 9, and the ESG score of the family business is obtained. The higher the score, the better the ESG performance of the family business.

**(3) Control variable**

Referring to the research of Liu and Cao (2015) and Huang and Liu (2020), the following control variables that may affect the cost of debt financing are added: Size, Lev, Cash, ROA, Growth, Fixed, Ind, Big1. Year and industry control variables are added. The definitions of the control variables are listed in Table 1.

### 3.3. Model

$$Cost_{i,t} = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 ESG_{i,t} + \alpha_2 Size_{i,t} + \alpha_3 Lev_{i,t} + \alpha_4 Cash_{i,t} + \alpha_5 ROA_{i,t} + \alpha_6 Growth_{i,t} + \alpha_7 Fixed_{i,t} + \alpha_8 Ind_{i,t} + \alpha_9 Big1_{i,t} + \sum Industry + \sum Year + \psi_{i,t}$$ (1)

In Formula 1, $\alpha_1$ represents the impact of the ESG performance (ESG) of the family business on the cost of debt financing (Cost).

## 5. Robustness test

### 5.1. Endogenous

Second, there may be a causal reverse relationship between ESG and debt financing costs. Based on this, fixed effect method, lag explanatory variable method and 2SLS method are used to alleviate the endogenous problems caused by missing variables and reverse causality. The regression results are shown in Table 4. From column (1) of Table 4, it can be seen that after using fixed effect regression to control individual and time fixed effect, the impact coefficient of ESG on Cost is -0.001, and it is significant at the level of 1%. The regression results in column (2) of Table 4 show that ESG can still significantly reduce the debt financing cost after the explanatory variable lags behind for one period. Referring to Benlemlih and Bitar (2018) who used the industry average ESG score (ESG_ind) and the enterprise initial ESG score (ESG_0) as the instrument variables of ESG, the regression results of the first stage showed that the two instrument variables were significantly positively correlated with ESG, and the ESG forecast value of the second stage significantly negatively affected the Cost, with a coefficient of -0.001, which was significant at the level of 5%.

### 6.2. Impact of family life cycle

Family businesses in different life cycles have different attitudes towards social emotional wealth and pay different attention to stakeholders. Therefore, the impact of ESG conducted by family businesses in different life cycles on corporate financing costs may be different. The life cycle of family business is mainly divided into two stages: Family sustainable operation and Family exit. Referring to the research of Liu and Liu (2012), the family sustainable operation is further divided into owner management stage, child’s cultivation and development stage, father-son partnership stage and power transfer stage. When the family is in the stage of owner management, no second generation of the family enters the enterprise; When the family is in the stage of children’s cultivation and development, the second generation of the family serves as senior executives, but does not serve as chairman or CEO; When the family is in the stage of father-son partnership, the second generation of the family becomes the right-hand man of the first generation of the family. Generally, the father acts as the chairman and the children act as CEO. When the family is in the stage of power transmission, the parents withdraw, the children succeed, and the first and second generations of the family complete the transfer of power. When the enterprise is in the family exit stage, the actual controller changes, including the sale of the ownership of the enterprise by the family owner and the withdrawal of family members from management (Yu and Wang, 2016).

According to the above definition, the sample of family businesses are divided into five groups according to the life cycle of family businesses, and tested respectively. It can be seen from Table 7 that the impact of ESG on Cost is significant in the owner management stage, the child’s cultivation and development stage, and the power transmission stage, with influence coefficients of -0.002, -0.001, and -0.002 respectively. The impact of ESG on Cost is not significant in the father-son partnership stage and the family exit stage. When the family business is in the owner management stage, the family generation has high human capital, and the family generation’s years of business management experience has been recognized by the market. At this time, ESG can significantly reduce the debt financing cost. When the family business is in the stage of children’s cultivation and development, there are two generations of family as senior managers in the business. The family’s ability and willingness to protect and enhance social emotional wealth are relatively strong. The family business ESG is considered altruistic, indicating that it pays more attention to long-term interests, so the performance of ESG can significantly reduce the debt financing cost. When the family business is in the stage of father-son partnership, the two generations of father and son form a conflict of values due to differences in educational background and overseas experience. At this time, the enterprise may have internal family conflicts (Cheng, 2018), internal conflicts within family businesses can weaken the effectiveness of ESG. When the family business is in the stage of power transmission, the second generation of the family officially takes over, the first generation of the family fades out of the business, and the contradictions and conflicts within the family business no longer exist. The positive ESG actions of the second generation of the family show the determination of the second generation to actively manage the business and promote the sustainable development of the business. At this time, ESG performance can reduce the cost of debt financing. When the family is in the family exit stage, the family business gradually gives up the management right and control right, and the long-term orientation of the family business no longer exists. At this time, ESG is more likely to behave as opportunistic behavior of the management, and ESG performance will not reduce the debt financing cost.

## 7. Conclusion

This paper studies whether the performance of ESG affects the cost of debt financing based on the sample of China’s Shanghai and Shenzhen A-share listed family enterprises from 2009 to 2021. The results show that the performance of ESG can reduce the cost of debt financing. Further study found that the performance of S and G had a significant impact on the cost of financing; The impact of ESG performance on debt financing costs is significant in the owner management stage, the children’s cultivation and development stage, and the power transfer stage.
