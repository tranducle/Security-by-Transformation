### ABSTRACT
Despite several research evaluating the connection between sustainability and the bottom line of firms, the existing research is inconclusive. In addition, most studies focus on multinational firms based in the United States or Europe, while research in the context of emerging economies is scant. This paper endeavours to investigate the strength of the association between Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) disclosure scores and the financial performance of 272 Indian firms listed on National Stock Exchange for the period 2015 to 2021 by employing panel data regression techniques. The results indicate that organisations having better ESG performance outperform their industry peers financially and are valued more by the market. Moreover, the data indicates that financial markets for lenders consider ESG disclosure when determining creditworthiness. In accordance with this, businesses can examine their operations and become more aligned with eco-friendly methods. Even though ESG disclosure appears to be associated with improved financial success, this is not the case across all industries and during COVID-19 pandemic. This study is relevant due to the growing significance of sustainability and the role of emerging nations in international commerce. It helps managers prioritize resource allocations to ESG-related activities that may affect financial performance differently across industry sectors, thus adding to the literature on the financial effects of ESG disclosures, particularly industry-specific aspects. This research represents a pioneering effort by assessing the impact of ESG disclosure on the profitability, market value, and cost of financing of Indian firms using both aggregation and disaggregation methods.

## 1. Introduction

Modern sustainable development has centred around ensuring the long-term viability and sustainability of the financial system (Aras et al., 2018). Businesses are facing enormous pressure to succeed and retain their success as markets have become more intense and dynamic. Thus, sustainability is essential for organisations and for overall economic growth. Corporate sustainability has recently received a lot of attention as businesses, investors, and consumers have grown more conscious of its significance (Maji and Lohia, 2023). Companies have repeatedly taken decisions that boost their short-term profits despite their severe negative impact on the environment, society, and governance. However, in the modern era, they are expected to consider environmental and social sustainability in addition to their limited, short-term profit maximization goals (Khandelwal et al., 2023). Future market leadership is increasingly becoming a must rather than an option. Sustainable practices focus on transforming corporations into responsible organisations that care about the environment and the social aspects (Alshehhi et al., 2018). Around 3800 institutions worldwide consider Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) issues during the decision-making process as of March 2020, compared to a merely 734 in 2010 (Yuan et al., 2022). As a comprehensive framework, United Nations (UN) member states adopted an “Agenda for Sustainable Development” in 2015 consisting of 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) anticipated to be achieved by the year 2030 (United Nations, 2020).

Sustainability has gained prominence due to the drastic growth of international financial markets. Firms must concentrate on increasing their revenue and being reputed corporate citizens. They are exposed to various types of risks such as market risk, business risk and operational risk undermining their profitability and endurance which can be mitigated to some extent by participating in sustainable endeavours. Negligence towards sustainability can result in serious reputational damage, sharp drops in stock prices, loss of business and inability to attract young talent. The significance of financial decision-making in business is evident since many elements that lead to failure can be overseen appropriately with strategies and choices that drive growth and development. Sustainability disclosure has become prominent among listed firms since the inception of the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) in 2001 (Sharma et al., 2020). Given the difficulties posed by COVID-19, the ability of sustainable disclosures is more vital than ever as it illuminates how companies can strengthen their non-financial reporting. The trust of the various investors and stakeholders can be bolstered in this unpredictable market through the quality and transparency of the information supplied.

ESG is anticipated to have three distinct effects on companies: lowering agency costs, lessening of informational gaps, and an improvement in reputation (Houqe et al., 2020). Non-financial disclosures have been hypothesised to have beneficial effects on businesses, including elevating corporate performance (Carnini Pulino et al., 2022), boosting reputation, and lowering the cost of capital. Even after recognizing the need to address sustainability, businesses prefer to halt significant changes to their business practices due to the lack of clarity regarding the benefits derived from sustainable practices (Bocken and Geradts, 2020). By presenting fresh empirical evidence, this work aims to clarify the uncertainty.

This study is a departure from and an extension of prior studies in several ways. Firstly, it explores the relationship between ESG disclosures and firm performance using diverse financial performance metrics, with a specific focus on an emerging market. This contrasts with earlier research, which predominantly concentrates on developed nations (Ademi and Klungseth, 2022; Hamrouni et al., 2020; Alareeni and Hamdan, 2020). In comparison to developed countries, ESG disclosures are so far voluntary and not widely prevalent in the Indian context (Hasan et al., 2022), highlighting the need for further exploration. Secondly, the impact of ESG disclosure may differ across firms, contingent upon the industry-specific context. However, the question of how ESG disclosure affects financial outcomes in different industry sectors—whether positively, insignificantly, or negatively—remains unexplored. Existing literature highlights the need for further research with a more robust analysis across various industries (Arian et al., 2022; Omar and Zallom, 2016). Therefore, our study employs disaggregate analysis based on industry type, offering a comprehensive perspective on the dynamics between ESG disclosure and financial performance. Additionally, it has been argued that companies disclosing superior ESG performance not only strengthen their bonds with stakeholders, acting as a protective shield during unforeseen crises (Hwang et al., 2021), but also receive recognition from investors, enabling them to navigate crises more effectively than their counterparts (Ademi and Klungseth, 2022). However, the relation between ESG disclosure and a firm’s financial and market performance during unexpected crises remains unexplored. This study aims to fill this research gap by providing new empirical evidence on the influence of ESG disclosure on financial performance, market valuation, and cost of debt during 2015–2021, which encompasses the COVID-19 pandemic period.

The study contributes to the current body of literature in several ways. It concentrates on a comprehensive measure of ESG disclosure along with isolating the impact of its individual dimensions. The findings of this study help strengthen the existing literature in the field of finance by providing insight into the extent to which Indian listed companies disclose ESG information and into the relationship between ESG disclosure, firm performance, and the cost of capital. As a result, listed businesses would be better able to optimise their capital structures, strengthen the credibility of their non-financial reporting, implement effective systems for corporate governance, improve profitability, and reduce their cost of capital.

The study examines a wide range of ESG indicators and the variations between diverse sectors, which, to our comprehension, has not yet been directly examined. This renders the outcomes of this paper important for bridging a gap in the academic literature on ESG in non-financial firms, with consequences for policymakers and policy implementers (e.g., companies and stakeholders). This examination assists researchers and businesses in discerning whether maintaining and disclosing exceptional ESG performance continues to impact a firm’s performance and valuation amidst unforeseen crises. The empirical results of our study will assist government officials and policymakers in better understanding how their policies impact the performance of firms, enabling them to make more informed decisions. The findings will also help business practitioners overcome fears about the potential negative effect of sustainability initiatives on firm performance, encouraging innovative and strategic changes in the business models to seize opportunities associated with sustainability. The findings will be useful for management team in making better financing decisions by revealing how ESG ratings affect growth and shareholder wealth. This will also enable the management to understand how ESG investments affect firm value, profitability, and financing dynamics. By recognizing the implications of ESG scores, the study will also help reduce a firm’s risk and improve its overall performance. Thus, the study intends to analyse and provide a case for value-added insights to investors and all stakeholders and contribute to the existing knowledge base on sustainability.

The remaining parts of the paper are organised as follows. Section 2 reviews extant literature including a concise outline of the theories related to stakeholders and agencies; Section 3 defines the research methodology; and Section 4 presents the results of the analysis. Additional analyses are presented in Section 5. Section 6 discusses our findings as well as their academic and policy implications; it concludes with suggestions for future research.

## 2. Theory and hypothesis development

The literature has been derived from a variety of neoclassical corporate finance and management theories. We will be anchoring on agency theory and stakeholder theory in order to develop our hypotheses. A company committed to sustainable efforts will include all stakeholders to contribute and increase business value (Xu et al., 2022). Pressure from the stakeholders is one of the primary reasons of businesses implementing and undertaking sustainable activities (Iyer and Jarvis, 2019; Xu et al., 2022). The stakeholder theory emphasizes the responsibilities of a firm towards its diverse stakeholders, encompassing both internal (customers and employees) and external stakeholders (suppliers, creditors, government etc.) (Al Barrak et al., 2023). ESG disclosures play a crucial role in disseminating information among these stakeholders, offering additional insights to support decision-making. They also significantly reduce uncertainty, enhance transparency, and effectively meet the interests and expectations of a broad spectrum of stakeholders (Hamrouni et al., 2019). According to the theory, stakeholders have charge of vital resources, thus businesses must maintain positive relationships with them. Agency theory (Jensen, 1986; Jensen and Meckling, 1976) suggests that lenders (principals) provide funding to a business (agent) with an expectation of return on their capital and risk-adjusted interest (Al Barrak et al., 2023). The theory assumes an information asymmetry that benefits agents, possibly leading them to prioritize their interests due to this unequal access to information. Since lenders have limited access to a firm’s private data, they rely on publicly available information to assess potential financial risks (Al Barrak et al., 2023). Comprehensive disclosures by companies help in mitigating information asymmetry and the resulting agency costs stemming from the separation of ownership and control (Carnini Pulino et al., 2022). Within this framework, ESG disclosures serve as a monitoring tool, reducing agency conflicts by providing information to external stakeholders (Hamrouni et al., 2019).

### 2.1. Evolution of ESG in India

The UN SDGs offer a holistic framework addressing environmental, social and economic challenges. Globally adopted sustainability reporting frameworks like Global Reporting Initiative (GRI), Integrated Reporting (IR) and Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB) provide guidance to businesses on measuring, monitoring and disclosing performance in ESG areas, adhering to specific key performance indicators (KPIs) and principles (PwC, 2021). India, housing a substantial portion of the world’s population, is a prominent emerging economy with a consistent annual GDP growth rate averaging 6.21% from 2006 to 2023, with the highest being 9.10% in 2022 (Trading Economics, 2023). This growth trajectory, amongst other factors such as a favourable business environment, regulated framework, and government initiatives promoting and enforcing ESG disclosure norms, has attracted the attention and investment of foreign investors (Doshi et al., 2024). Over the past decade, India has witnessed a remarkable evolution in ESG reporting, catalyzed by the introduction of mandatory Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)[^1] regulations and SEBI’s mandates for ESG related disclosures. Beginning 2009, India laid the groundwork for ESG reporting with the issuance of the National Voluntary Guidelines (NVGs) on Corporate Social Responsibility by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA, 2011), later strengthened by SEBI’s regulatory frameworks like the Green Debt Securities Circular in 2017. The reporting landscape evolved, mandating the Business Responsibility Report (BRR) based on NVGs in 2012 for the top 100 listed companies by market capitalization, expanding to the top 500 in 2015 (PwC, 2021). With the introduction of the Companies Act 2013, CSR was mandated in 2014. In 2017, SEBI recommended the voluntary adoption of IR for the top 500 companies preparing BRR (PwC, 2021). In 2019, the National Guidelines on Responsible Business Conduct (NGRBC) were introduced, and SEBI extended the BRR requirement to the top 1000 listed companies from FY 2019–20. These mandates mark a significant shift in reporting methods, responding to the trend in global practices. A substantial shift in the reporting methods has been witnessed recently, with SEBI’s transition from BRR to Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR) in May 2021. It mandates ESG disclosures for the top 1000 listed companies, expanding reporting beyond financial aspects to encompass environmental, social and governance factors, compelling firms to holistically engage with stakeholders (PwC, 2021). Introduced on a voluntary basis in 2021–2022, the BRSR framework was made mandatory from 2022 to 2023 (SEBI, 2021), promoting responsible business practices and aligning with global sustainability goals.

Moreover, in the current scenario, ESG has gained significance due to several factors. India’s vision of achieving a US$5 trillion economy by 2024–25 may face challenges without substantial foreign direct investments (FDI), necessitating continuous high-level disclosure of firm performance in terms of non-finance metrics (Maji and Lohia, 2023). Secondly, the increasing popularity of ESG in developed nations is compelling their developing counterparts to prioritize sustainability. For example, in 2021, the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed mandatory climate-related disclosure (KPMG, 2021), a development with notable significance for India, given that the USA was the second-largest investor country with 18% FDI inflows in India during FY 2021–2022 (Maji and Lohia, 2023). Therefore, addressing ESG issues and disclosure becomes imperative for the uninterrupted flow of FDI, particularly for India being a significant greenhouse gas emitter.

### 2.2. ESG and firm profitability

Over the past decade, the relationship between ESG disclosure (ESGD) and financial performance (FP) has been extensively investigated, with the majority of studies indicating a favourable relationship between ESGD and FP (Maji and Lohia, 2023). The positive association is predominantly substantiated by stakeholder theory, which posits that investment in ESG activities elevates a firm’s market reputation (Ademi and Klungseth, 2022). This, in turn, generates increased demand for a firm’s products, thereby positively influencing its financial performance (Behl et al., 2022). Moreover, ESGD aligns with the expectations of diverse stakeholders, including the government. This proactive approach aids in mitigating potential regulatory litigations related to ESG requirements, preventing future financial burdens and consequently contributing to a positive impact on the firm’s overall financial performance (Behl et al., 2022; Ademi and Klungseth, 2022). Bansal et al. (2021) recognised three research streams. Each stream claims that ESG and financial success are positively, negatively, or not correlated. First-stream scholars such as McGuire et al. (1988) claim that being socially and ecologically responsible is not expensive and corporations can benefit monetarily from their duties, showing a positive correlation between ESGD and FP and indicating that better ESG performance increases market value. Alareeni and Hamdan (2020), Bahadori et al. (2021) and Jaisinghani and Sekhon (2022) confirm the beneficial association between ESG disclosure and financial performance through empirical research (Sandberg et al., 2023). Disclosure of ESG data has been shown to improve business performance by decreasing agency costs (Maroun, 2015) and bridging the information gap between management and investors. Despite the prevailing agreement regarding the positive link between ESGD and FP, empirical results regarding the relationship have been mixed. According to Sekhon and Kathuria (2019) and Fahad and Busru (2021), two studies that conclude the second line of inquiry (negative correlation), social and environmental responsibility is costly. According to Yoon et al. (2018), an increase in sustainability disclosure could reduce financial performance because it raises the costs for firms; Oware and Mallikarjunappa (2022) document similar result. On the contrary, Bansal et al. (2021) conclude that there is no association between ESGD and financial performance. Hasan et al. (2022) state that the effect of CSR disclosure on FP differs by industry type and financial performance metric.

Built upon stakeholder theory, we propose the following hypothesis regarding the relationship between ESGD and firm performance:

**Hypothesis 1.** ESG disclosures have a positive impact on firm’s profitability.

### 2.3. ESG and firm value

Several researchers have explored the relationship between ESG rating and corporate value across industries and nations, but their findings remain inconsistent (Behl et al., 2022). Some research supports the agency problem hypothesis, which states that insiders often prioritize their own interests over those of the shareholders by placing too much emphasis on ESG-related activities (Buchanan et al., 2018). As a result, there is a negative correlation between ESG and stock prices when excessive capital is invested (Gillan et al., 2021). Aligned with stakeholder theory, investing in ESG initiatives and demonstrating superior ESG performance enable firms to develop tangible and intangible assets, enhancing operational efficiency, product quality and reputation (Ademi and Klungseth, 2022). Higher ESGD is also perceived positively by stakeholders, fostering their trust (Bansal et al., 2021). These factors collectively contribute to a strengthened competitive advantage, ultimately increasing the firm’s value (Behl et al., 2022). Thus, ESG activities serve a dual role in generating value for firms: firstly, by boosting shareholder’s value through increased cash flows (driven by improved product sales due to enhanced productivity and positive reputation) and secondly, by maximizing shareholders utility derived from owning shares in a sustainable firm (Abdi et al., 2022). Based on stakeholder theory, we propose the following hypothesis:

**Hypothesis 2.** ESG disclosures have a positive effect on the market value of firms.

### 2.4. ESG and cost of debt

Companies worldwide participate in environmental, social, and governance initiatives for various reasons, the most important of which is to gain preferential treatment in the financial markets (Cheng et al., 2014).

Several studies conclude that firms that do better in terms of CSR have fewer capital constraints (Cheng et al., 2014; Chan et al., 2017; Hamrouni et al., 2019). Better CSR performance is linked to higher stakeholder engagement, which lowers possible agency costs. Second, corporations with a strong track record of corporate social responsibility are more likely to report their activities in the public sphere, making them more visible and accountable. Increased transparency reduces informational inequalities between the firm and investors, lowering perceived risk. Limkriangkrai et al. (2017) find that Australian firms with high ESG ratings have a higher level of leverage. Yeh et al. (2020) investigate the impact of CSR practices on Chinese firms and conclude that firms that do more CSR could quickly lower their cost of debt capital. Thus, firms can use CSR to minimize their cost of debt by communicating to creditors about their commitment to social responsibility and sustainable development (Lemma et al., 2022).

A lender’s ability to recognize factors influencing a borrower’s capacity to repay loans is essential to effective lending practices. Maintaining the legitimacy and improving its reputation is critical to firms for their survival and sustainability, especially in an intense and dynamic market where reputation can significantly impact their economic value. The credibility of ESG disclosure in lowering financing costs is determined by whether it can attract the attention of lenders. If ESG disclosures appeal to the lenders by supplying them with appropriate non-financial measure, a reduction in information asymmetry between lenders and firms could be achieved by firm’s commitment to ESG disclosure on a wide scale.

The findings of Magnanelli and Izzo (2017) demonstrate a positive relationship between corporate social performance (CSP) and borrowing costs, indicating that CSR does not drive value for French firms. A high CSP is not seen as a risk reducing factor by banks. The findings imply that oftentimes, financial institutions see CSR operations as either an unnecessary and costly drain on resources or, even worse, a risky endeavour. This finding is consistent with earlier research on CSR overinvestment, which shows how executives invest heavily in CSR programmes for personal gain rather than requirements of shareholders (Goss and Roberts, 2011).

Eliwa et al. (2021) point out that businesses in EU countries can gain from enhancing ESG performance and transparency as lending institutions will charge lesser cost of capital. Kordsachia (2021), Sandra et al. (2021) and Houqe et al. (2020) show that creditors favor non-financial disclosure by lowering the required rate of return, thus suggesting a negative relationship between the cost of debt and overall ESG performance of firms.

Gracia and Siregar (2021) find some evidence in the ASEAN countries and conclude that sustainability policies reduce the cost of debt for firms. Even though debt holders consider sustainability practices of firms (as assessed by disclosure) when evaluating their creditworthiness, they do not consider sustainable performance scores to be relevant. This could be related to the fact that in contrast to sustainability performance scores, certain crucial information is more visible in sustainability disclosure.

From the perspective of agency theory, better disclosure of ESG practices holds the potential to lower a firm’s cost of debt by significantly mitigating information asymmetry (Atan et al., 2018). This could lead to favourable credit ratings and a reduction in perceived risk among lenders (Cheng et al., 2014). In the context of stakeholder theory, ESGD is anticipated to strengthen the relationship of the firm with its stakeholders, including lenders. This strengthened relationship may result in improved access to debt at favourable rates for firms with better ESGD scores (Hamrouni et al., 2019). Grounded upon the principles of stakeholder theory, the proposed hypothesis is:

**Hypothesis 3.** ESG disclosures have a negative effect on the cost of debt of firms.

## 3. Research methodology

### 3.1. Sample and data

The initial sample includes 500 Indian companies listed on the National Stock Exchange (NIFTY 500) from 2015 to 2021. The research period starts with the enactment of the laws mandating the disclosure of CSR information, which came into effect in 2014-15 and the availability of ESG data for NSE 500 firms. Financial enterprises were excluded from the initial population due to diverse accounting policies, sector specific disclosure practices and different regulatory constraints (Khandelwal et al., 2023; Borralho et al., 2022). Additionally, firms with missing data were omitted from the sample. The final sample thus comprises of 272 Indian listed organisations, as presented in Panel A of Table 1.

Additionally, to investigate the industrial influence on the link between ESG disclosure and financial performance of the firms, the associations have been analysed across industry categories offered by the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS). The data for the study has been sourced from the Bloomberg® database because of their neutrality, dependability, and widespread adoption (e.g., Aragon-Correa et al., 2016; Baldini et al., 2018; Houqe et al., 2020). Due to the fact that not all companies reveal their ESG activities, the number of companies having ESG scores varies from year to year.

The distribution of the sample by industry sector is presented in Panel B of Table 1. The sample is fairly representative of the rest of the economy, including large numbers of observations from materials, industrials, consumer discretionary, and healthcare.

### 3.2. Variables and measurement

As a result, the success of a company is evaluated using three criteria: profitability (as proxied by ROA), firm value (as proxied by Tobin’s Q), and cost of capital (as proxied by COD). ESG ratings from Bloomberg measure the independent variable. This study employs a number of control variables to improve the internal validity of the analysis. The natural logarithm of total assets is used to assess a firm’s size since organisations of different sizes approach sustainability differently. Large organisations are likely to utilise economies of scale and scope. Having a higher operational efficiency and a stronger financial position results in enhanced financial performance. Firm leverage, a proxy for unsystematic risk, is used because organisations with a larger leverage ratio may have more financial risk and inferior financial performance.

## 4. Results and analysis

This section presents the association between ESG disclosure scores and firm performance. The impact of ESG scores along with their individual dimensions (E, S and G) on the profitability of firms has been examined initially. Second, the study explores how ESG score and its constituents (E, S, and G) affect firm value. Lastly, the impact of ESG score on the cost of debt of firms is investigated. LM test and Hausman test are used to determine the most accurate estimator among pooled OLS, fixed effect model and random effect model.

### 4.3. Impact of ESG disclosure on cost of debt

Table 6 displays the outcomes of plugging in values for the ESG disclosure score and its sub-components into Equation 3. The results demonstrate that the aggregate ESG scores have a significant negative impact on the cost of debt. The social dimension and governance dimension have a large negative impact on the cost of debt, while the environment score has a considerable positive impact. In accordance with our premise, the statistics indicate that businesses with better ESG disclosure witness less cost of debt. This implies that lending institutions include ESG disclosure data in their risk assessment models. The findings indicate that lenders view environmental disclosure as an unnecessary drain of business resources or, even worse, as a potential risk-increasing activity as concluded by Magnanelli and Izzo (2017). However, the inverse relationship between governance and social constituents with cost of debt may ameliorate the weak relationship between environmental factors and the cost of debt. When making loan decisions, lending institutions evaluate the reliability and credibility of a firm’s management team based on the combined impact of the various ESG factors. In general, these results are compatible with hypotheses regarding ESG practises supporting the premise that ESG practises increase the financial performance of organisations (Hamrouni et al., 2019; Eliwa et al., 2021).

In terms of control variables, the data reveals a negative relationship of the cost of debt with size, ROA, and interest coverage ratio. Larger businesses are more resistant to negative cash flow shocks and less likely to fail. Moreover, reputation is positively associated with the size of business that may convince investors that larger organisations are less risky (Goss and Roberts, 2011). Due to the lower likelihood of default and economies of scale, larger businesses acquire external financing at cheaper interest rates. ROA exhibits a significant negative regression coefficient. Consistent with the pecking order theory, our findings suggest that organisations with high profitability and growth opportunities prefer internal sources of finance and are thus less reliant on debt. Greater expected profitability will lessen the company’s vulnerability to financial market volatility. Profitable businesses are more likely to pay their liabilities on time stemming from debt incurred or debt securities issued. Businesses with adequate financial liquidity have the required quantity of assets to regularly meet their present obligations. Therefore, financial institutions and investors provide a reduced liquidity risk premium to these organisations. A company’s interest coverage ratio (ICR) indicates how well it can meet its interest payment obligations. Thus, there is less chance of default and a larger capacity to pay interest on debt obligations for companies that are larger, more profitable, have a lower interest coverage ratio, and generate more cash from operations than their peers.

## 5. Additional analyses

### 5.1. Disaggregation of industries

According to institutional theory, the institutional settings (such as the industry) in which a business operates have an impact on the sustainability practises employed by firms (Gracia and Siregar, 2021). Additionally, NIFTY500 is a widely followed stock market index that includes companies from various sectors. Consequently, it is crucial to analyse the relationships for particular industries rather than as a group. Morgan Stanley Capital International (MSCI) and Standard & Poor’s have provided a Global Industry Classification Standard® disaggregation as per various industries, which has been utilised to provide a thorough explanation and comprehension of above findings. We classify businesses operating in the energy, materials, and utility sectors as environmentally sensitive industries in line with Yoon et al. (2018) and Gracia and Siregar (2021). Firms in sensitive industries are required to adhere to more stringent disclosure requirements due to their potentially detrimental activities (Yoon et al., 2018). Concurrently, these firms are required to achieve superior corporate performance compared to their counterparts in non-sensitive industries. The impact of ESG disclosure scores on a firm’s cost of debt, Tobin’s Q, and ROA is reported in Panel A, B, and C of appendix Table A3 respectively. The results show a significant negative impact of ESG disclosure scores on the cost of debt for firms operating in Communication services, Consumer discretionary, Consumer staples, Energy, Healthcare, Industrials, Information technology and Real estate industries. There is no significant relationship in materials and utilities sector. Since these industries are more likely to be criticised for their environmental impact, it is possible that these companies derive less value from their sustainable efforts. Even if firms in sensitive industries implement sustainability practises more effectively than firms in non-sensitive industries, it is possible that these practises are insufficient to meet the expectations of debtholders about the long-term viability. To overcome this obstacle, companies in these sectors need to have effective means of spreading the word about their initiatives.

### 5.2. Alternate measures

The robustness of the findings is evaluated using various performance measurements. Return on equity (ROE), an additional measure of firm-level performance, is added to the analysis in order to validate the outcomes of the preliminary tests related to hypothesis 1. Market to book ratio (MTB) is a robustness measure in order to validate the findings of hypothesis 2. Findings have been reported in appendix Table A4. We observe a significant positive relationship between ESG scores and ROE, as well as ESG scores and market to book ratio, which is consistent with the preliminary analytical conclusions.

## 6. Discussion and conclusion

### 6.1. Discussion

The findings indicate that ESG disclosure scores have a positive impact on the financial performance (as proxied by return on assets), market valuation (as proxied by Tobin’s Q) and the cost of debt for firms, indicating that a stronger corporate performance can be attained by improving sustainability disclosure. The reason for this can be attributed to the fact that businesses with greater sustainable initiatives are able to successfully minimize conflicts among stakeholders and match their business objectives, increasing financial performance. Existing literature on the relation between ESG and financial performance has yet to reach a consensus. In addition, most of the studies make conclusions based on either cross-sectoral data or specific index. This disregards the fact that each industry is vulnerable to different sustainability risks.

Evaluation of environmental policies and plans by any third-party boosts return on assets and productivity of firms, improving their efficiency and profitability. Consequently, environmental policies impact the market value of businesses. It is believed that social practises and activities stimulate corporate efficiency. In addition, the findings of this study demonstrate that participation in social activities generates higher financial performance by having a favourable relationship with ROA and market value. These findings validate the prevalence of the stakeholder theory. Investment in environmental, social, and governance (ESG) activities tends to pay off for businesses because it attracts more investors to their company, which leads to more business activity, more investable money, and more cash flow. Addressing the needs and demands of stakeholders through enhanced ESG disclosure, rather than solely focusing on profit maximization, provides a competitive advantage to the firm over industry peers. Thus, firms that disclose their social, environmental, and sustainability performance foster a relationship with their stakeholders that is mutually beneficial and, in turn, boosts the company’s bottom line.

From the findings related to the impact of ESG disclosure on the cost of debt, it can be inferred that businesses that are more transparent about their sustainability efforts, pay less interest on their debt because their lenders factor in information related to sustainability practices while assessing their risk and creditworthiness. Aligned with stakeholder theory, firms embracing ESG practices may garner increased support and trust from lenders, securing favourable treatment and more affordable debt financing. This is facilitated by the dual impact of ESG disclosure in reducing information asymmetry and helping lending institutions in assessing default risk. The findings indicate that lenders appreciate enhanced disclosures, rewarding companies through reduced capital constraints and the cost of debt. This underscores the crucial role played by the market in incentivizing firms to adopt and effectively communicate ESG practices.

The study establishes that superior environmental disclosure significantly influences profitability, market value and the cost of debt for Indian firms. Environmental disclosure incorporates pertinent indicators that alleviate doubts among investors and lenders, especially concerning sensitivity to ecological issues. Consequently, investors and lenders develop favourable perceptions towards these firms, recognizing that environmental activities are not a costly diversion of firm resources. Firms in emerging markets should realize the risk-reducing benefits of adopting environment friendly practices and consider redesigning their policies, processes and overall governance practices accordingly.

Managers should focus on increasing and disseminating information related to ESG, as it has the potential to improve financial performance and lower the cost of financing. In light of the growing emphasis on ESG issues by investors and lending institutions, managers should consider adopting good governance mechanisms and investing further in sustainable practices. As an effective communication tool, ESG disclosure prompts managers to expand communication channels, moving beyond traditional reports to include alternate channels like corporate websites and press releases. This expansion enhances the accessibility of ESG information, making it easier for investors and lending institutions to access real-time information at lower costs.

Consistent with the previous literature (Chen et al., 2023; Tanjung, 2023; Yuen et al., 2022), this study reinforces the adverse impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on firm performance and the dynamics of financing. ESG disclosure, when moderated with a COVID dummy, has not been shown to mitigate the impact of the pandemic significantly. During the COVID-19 pandemic, adopting and disclosing ESG practices became more costly, leading to increased expenses and a subsequent decline in revenue and profitability. Firms’ stock prices experienced a significant downturn due to the pandemic, leading to a decrease in market valuation. This supports the traditional neo-classical hypothesis and agency theory, suggesting that implementing ESG practices during a crisis consumes resources and intensifies agency conflicts, which ultimately leads to a decline in the profitability of firms. Lenders also dismiss ESG practices in times of crisis, perceiving them as a strategy to conceal poor performance. As a result, the usual benefits associated with ESG disclosures during stable periods diminish in times of crisis, resulting in a higher cost of borrowing. Thus, unlike developed countries where the impact of ESG disclosure is more pronounced (Lu and Khan, 2023), lending institutions and investors in emerging nations like India, currently do not recognize the financial advantage associated with sustainability practices and give lesser priority to sustainability disclosures during times of crisis.

Several countries are proclaiming future pledges to fight the deteriorating environmental effects. Given their strong growth rates, emerging countries assume greater significance for meeting these obligations. Due to their rapid growth and inadequate internal resources, firms in developing markets generally have a greater requirement for external capital than those in industrialised nations. Consequently, the cost of financing is particularly crucial for companies operating in developing markets. As an emerging economy, there are compelling reasons to perform research for Indian firms. To begin with, the Indian government, under the Companies Act 2013, has made it essential for listed firms in India to disclose CSR-related information if specific conditions are met (Hasan et al., 2022). The Indian government has consistently encouraged firms to have sound corporate governance practices in place and focus on sustainability concerns by making it mandatory for specified companies to disclose sustainability data beginning from the financial year 2023 under BRSR. This is also consistent with the goal of the Indian government to meet its pledge of attaining net zero emissions by 2070 (Nandi, 2022). ESG reporting is essential for a developing economy like India because it enables to develop a comprehensive system of economic and sustainable measurement that goes beyond traditional financial-centric models.

### 6.2. Conclusion

Sustainable development initiatives are gradually transforming the corporate paradigm. Consequently, it should reduce the climate-damaging effects of doing business and benefit all the stakeholders. Due to widespread social disturbances and climate change, businesses globally are confronting unprecedented challenges. Instead of ignoring the disruption, regulators and policy makers must constantly encourage industries to respond and adopt better practices. This study contributes to the body of knowledge by understanding the relationship between ESG disclosure scores and financial performance for Indian listed companies between the years 2015 and 2021 using a theoretical framework of agency theory and stakeholder theory. In order to determine whether disclosure of one or more ESG components is reflected more prominently in the financial and market performance of firms, the impact of each of the three ESG component is examined. Additionally, the study assesses how the impact of ESG varies across industries. Results indicate a significant positive relationship between aggregate and individual dimensions of ESG disclosure scores and firm profitability (ROA) as well as market value (Tobin’s Q). With respect to financing costs, ESG disclosure scores have a significant negative relationship with cost of debt for Indian firms, suggesting that lenders regard high sustainable disclosures as a characteristic that minimises the likelihood of firms facing legal issues, such as litigation/compliance expenses or other costs related to the environment. They, therefore view enterprises with better disclosures as less risky. However, in times of crisis, ESG practices are seen as an additional cost, increasing risk of lenders and hence the cost of debt. These findings signify the initial stages of development of sustainable finance in India characterised by lack of confidence among stakeholders, particularly investors and lenders.

The most important finding of our study is the empirical evidence that the above relationships vary across different industries. This study demonstrates that ESG performance does not benefit firms across all industries equally (Omar and Zallom, 2016; Arian et al., 2022), thus it is essential to evaluate ESG performance in a manner consistent with the industry. These findings suggest that managers should analyse the features of their respective industries and the importance of ESG performance to their financial performance. The disparities between industries should lead to the selection of diverse organisational strategies (Ortas et al., 2015).

In terms of theoretical implications, this study extends the current literature by exploring relationships involving non-financial disclosure, firm performance, firm value and cost of financing. Additionally, the study explores the impact of the global crises triggered by the COVID-19 pandemic, offering empirical evidence in an unexplored area. Moreover, the study provides insights into the contrasting perspectives of agency and stakeholder theories, supporting the complementary viewpoint with empirical evidence.

In terms of managerial implications, the research demonstrates that managers and decision-makers can favourably impact the financial success and market valuation of firms by turning their attention to ESG performance and disclosure. Focusing on enhancing ESG disclosure relative to industry peers could increase the profitability of a firm and increase its market value. Given the importance of ESG in lending decisions, managers should consider additional investments in sustainable practices to enhance reliability and reduce cost of financing. As a result, the findings of this study imply that managers can strategically leverage sustainable initiatives to lower information asymmetries. Consequently, decision-makers should prioritize allocating resources to sustainable activities, which helps in building rapport and confidence with stakeholders, proving beneficial in both stable and crisis situations.

This research has suggestions for investors as well. Enterprises operating in emerging countries can reduce their financing cost through sustainable activities. A firm’s access to reduced cost financing can be viewed as a factor contributing to its financial performance. Investors nowadays consider ESG reports into account when making investment decisions. Since organisations with better ESG performance usually have a greater market valuation, incorporating the firm’s ESG performance into investment decisions could lead to improved returns for the investors.

Lastly, for policymakers and regulators, the findings emphasize the significance of implementing a robust legal framework and vigilant oversight. To drive firms towards greater sustainability, regulators should be persistent in supporting and strengthening ESG initiatives by offering guidance, assistance and financial grants to firms. Ultimately, compliance and disclosure of ESG benefits society as firms contribute value to the environment and community at large.

While this study sheds new light on the relationship between ESG practices and the financial performance of firms, it has a number of shortcomings that suggest directions for future research. The ability to generalise the findings is hampered by the fact that the data for this study originate from a single country (India). Future research should broaden the sample to include more emerging economies with diverse cultural and institutional settings. The analysis focus on the quantity of ESG data revealed and not the quality. To remedy this, primary data can be acquired for further insights. A further constraint of the sample is that it is restricted to companies listed in Bloomberg database. This excludes numerous small and medium-sized firms that disclose ESG-related data but are unlisted. Consequently, if the sample had been broadened, more significant results could have been obtained.
